## Findings

Each finding is a checkbox. `must-fix` blocks `make review-pass` until a decompiler ticks it off
(with the commit that fixes it); `should` and `note` do not block.

- [ ] must-fix src/E19F3.C:36-39: the 006D clause says the rectangle "covers lines 191 to 199 (191 + 9 = 200),
  evidence for the [H] reading of y in lines". The bytes put 191 in y2 for 006D (082C:005B push 0xbf, then
  082C:0062 pushes [bp+c] as y1), but put 191 in y1 for 038A (082C:0383..0386). The same comment (lines 38-39)
  says the two callers disagree on which y origin holds 191. So "covers lines 191 to 199" holds only if y2 is
  the origin for 006D, which nothing shows, and the 191 + 9 = 200 evidence depends on that. State the condition,
  or drop the coverage claim and let the caps and region record 0 carry the [H] reading.

- [ ] must-fix src/E19F3.C:21-22: "a negative x1 can leave the same far edge, ClipRect(-30, 0, 0, 0, 20, 200, 0, 0)
  passes x2 = 30 and width = -10" presents the second example as the same quirk as the first. It is not. Its
  far edge is x2 + width = 20, inside the screen, and no origin reaches SCREEN_COLUMNS. What it shows is a
  negative width from a negative x1 (19F3:0036 adds x1 to the width and no test follows). The first example
  shows a different thing: x1 = 50 leaves the origin at or past SCREEN_COLUMNS, after the last x test (the QUIRK
  at 19F3:0045). Describe the two outcomes separately; the phrase "the same far edge" has no referent.

- [ ] should src/E19F3.C:24: "is lowered to 0 when negative" gives the wrong direction. A negative origin is raised
  to 0 (19F3:0034..0039: x1 = -30 becomes 0 and the 30 moves to x2). Say "raised to 0".

## Summary

The unit is understandable from its source alone. ClipRect names what the bytes do: it caps the size, moves a
negative origin to 0 with its partner origin and the size, and returns early when an x origin reaches
SCREEN_COLUMNS. The byte-level claims I checked are right. The two must-fix items are wrong claims in the
comments: the 006D rectangle's coverage depends on which y origin is the rectangle's, which the header itself
leaves open, and the second QUIRK example does not show the far-edge case it is cited for. The shoulds are the
direction word for the origin shift, the "pointer" reading of ds:8052 (a driver table slot [H] is the better
description), the missing whitepaper citation, the missing inline reason for the shifts, and the negative-size
QUIRK markers. Placeholders left: gFar8052 (named from its address) and extra1 and extra2 (roles unknown). Those
are honest.
